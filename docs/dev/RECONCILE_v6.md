## 2. Where the two rounds actually touch

Four files, and only four.

### `src/shared/Config.lua`
Both rounds add to it, in **disjoint blocks**. The progression round edits
button prices and `Layout`; the growth round adds `Config.Rebirth`,
`Config.Persistence`, `Config.UI`, `Config.Social`, `Config.Analytics` and
`Config.Waves` boss keys. No key is written by both.

### `tools/verify_config.lua`
The genuine contact point, and the one place a merge needs a human.

- The progression round **re-authored the floor-placement assertion**
  (`fraction >= 0.35 / <= 0.65` became `>= 0.06 / <= 0.20`) because moving
  `floor2` to minute 6 breaks the old band on purpose. **The growth round took
  their version wholesale** rather than writing a competing one.
- The growth round **replaced the rebirth check** in the block immediately
  below it. The two edits are adjacent and disjoint.
- Both rounds' checks sit inside the same economy simulation, so **the
  simulation itself must be merged once, not twice.**

### `build/PasteInto_*.lua`
Regenerated by `tools/verify.py` on every commit in both rounds, so **every
merge conflicts here mechanically.**

> **Resolution is always the same: take both `src/` sides, run
> `python3 tools/pack.py`, commit the result. Never hand-merge the generated
> files.**

### `src/server/Tycoon.lua`
The progression round fixed `powerFactor` and reworked the yard; the growth
round added the vault gauge inside `buildCollector` and one setter. Additive on
both sides, different functions.

## 4. The findings that crossed the line

Three things each round found that changed the other's work. They are the
argument for having done this coordination at all.

### The generator multiplied nothing — found by progression, verified by growth

`Tycoon.self.powerFactor` was initialised to `1`, reset to `1` on release and
rebirth, and **assigned nowhere else**. `INSTALLERS.Power` called
`refreshBeltSpeed()` without setting it. So the belt and every dropper ran at
stock speed from PR #32 onward.

Meanwhile `Tycoon:incomePerSecond` and `SessionService.incomePerSecondFor` both
multiplied by the correct derived `Config.powerFactor(has)`. **The plot quoted
up to 2× the income it produced** — on the HUD, on the buy button, and through
the offline mirror the growth round was about to switch on.

Two things worth keeping:

- **The verifier structurally could not catch it.** It reads `Config.lua` and
  nothing else, and every Config-level assertion about the power ladder passed.
- **HANDOFF_v5 §5 item 8 asked a human to check this exact number in Studio,
  named the two wrong answers it might be, and was asked twice and answered
  zero times. The real answer was a third thing neither round guessed: 37.**

This is why `#37` exists.

### `BaseCost` was not the lever — found by growth, changed nothing for progression

Sweeping `Config.Rebirth.BaseCost` from 0.5 to 900 seconds of endgame income
moves the first rebirth by about **twelve minutes across a 20× price cut**. It
is structurally pinned: `upgrader6` and `dropper10` multiply income ~17×
between them, so saving instead of buying them is never worth it.

**The lever that pulls the first rebirth earlier is the build length**, which
the progression round owned. `GROWTH-TODO.md` item 1 reads as though pricing
the pad is the fix; it is not. Their curve moved the pad from minute 60 to
minute 43. The growth round's contribution was making it a *fork* rather than a
wall, and asserting the outcome.

### A check that was not checking — found by progression, swept by growth

The distinct-surface-heights family contained `PathTopY = Config.World.PathTopY`
— a key that does not exist. The value was `nil`, so the entry never entered the
table, `pairs()` never visited it, and its own `type(y) == "number"` guard could
never fire. It covered three surfaces while appearing to cover four.

**Same shape as the generator bug: a thing that reads as checked and is not.**

Fixed in **#39 `power/yard-rework`** — *not* #38 — because that is where it was
tripped over: the surfaces check was supposed to be covering `YardTopY` and was
not. Branch by branch:

| branch | state |
| --- | --- |
| `pacing/early-mezzanine` (#36) | still live |
| `floors/ladder` (#38) | still live |
| `power/yard-rework` (#39) | **fixed** |
| `admin/commands` (#40) | fixed |

This is why **#43 was rebased onto #40** rather than left as a sibling of #38 —
a sibling of #38 would still have had the dead key underneath it.

The growth round swept `verify_config.lua` for the pattern — every
`X = Config.<path>` whose leaf never appears as an assignment in `Config.lua`.
**Result: a clean negative.** `PathTopY` was the only real one; the two other
hits were `PlotSize.X` / `.Z`, which are `Vector3` fields. So the pattern was a
one-off rather than endemic, and nobody needs to re-audit the other 1700 checks.

**Recommended for a later round:** that sweep is about fifteen lines of Python
and would make a reasonable seventh pass in `verify.py` — *"the verifier may not
reference a Config key that does not exist."* A check suite that can silently
not-check things is the one place a dead reference costs the most.

## 5. Things the next round should not re-litigate

- **`floor2` at minute 6 and the cabinets opening at minute 6 are deliberate.**
  This deletes the "twenty minutes of raids you cannot clear" window flagged in
  HANDOFF_v5 §5 item 4, which was an open problem, not a feature.
- **The floor-placement band is now 6–20% of the build, not 35–65%.** The old
  band measured a fraction of a clock that moves whenever anyone retunes
  anything; it went 49% → 62% in one round without `floor2`'s price changing at
  all.
- **`Config.Rebirth.BaseCost` is derived and must not be hand-set again.** The
  comment claiming it was "derived from endgame income" was false for two
  rounds. It is now actually derived, from `PriceRung` against the spine.
- **Graduating a prototype means deleting its flag**, not setting it `true`.
  `verify_config.lua` asserts every remaining flag is `false`.
